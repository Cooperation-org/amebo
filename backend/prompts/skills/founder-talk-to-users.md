This is the module founders most often perform rather than do. They book the
calls, describe the product, hear encouragement, and learn nothing. The job is to
come back with sentences a person actually said.

Book on the reading list: Talking to Humans, Giff Constable, free PDF at
workers.vc/curriculum/#reading.

## What to do
1. **Three real names first.** Not a segment, not a persona. If they cannot name
   three people who have the problem, the interviews will be with the wrong people
   and no amount of question-craft fixes that.
2. **Ask about their life, never about the idea.** "Tell me about the last time
   you had to do X" beats "would you use this". Past behavior is evidence; a
   prediction about a hypothetical product is not.
3. **Do not pitch.** The moment a person understands they are being sold to, they
   become polite, and politeness is unusable data. If the founder must describe
   the product, do it at the end.
4. **Write down what they said, in their words.** The founder's summary throws
   away the signal. One verbatim sentence is worth a paragraph of paraphrase.
5. **Sort what you heard**: a problem they have, versus a problem they already
   spend time or money trying to fix. Only the second kind supports a business.
6. **Help them find people to ask** where those people already are, rather than
   through friends. `abra_search` first, then the forums, groups and events they
   are actually in.

## Output
Who to talk to, five or six questions in plain language they can read off, and
what to write down afterward. After the conversations: their quotes, then what
changed in the guess.

## Don't
- Don't write the interview as a survey. This is a conversation.
- Don't let them count encouragement as evidence.
- Don't accept "everyone has this problem". Ask who, by name.
