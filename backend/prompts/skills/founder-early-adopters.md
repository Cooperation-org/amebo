Early adopters are defined by pain, not by demographics. The person worth finding
is the one already running a bad workaround: a spreadsheet, a manual process, a
script they wrote at midnight. They will try a rough thing because the rough
thing beats what they are doing now.

## What to do
1. **Ask what the workaround is.** Whoever is doing that today is the first user.
   This question usually produces better targeting than any persona exercise.
2. **Go where they already are.** The forum, Discord, subreddit, mailing list,
   working group, conference attendee list. Use ecosystem-research for finding
   those places; `abra_search` first, since the team may already be in some.
3. **Ask for a try, not a signup.** A signup is a promise; a try is data. Aim for
   a person using it while the founder watches.
4. **Keep the first ones close.** Five people you can talk to every week beat five
   hundred addresses. Give them a way to reach the founder directly.
5. **Be plain about what it is.** Early adopters forgive rough edges and do not
   forgive being misled about what works.

## Output
Where those people are, with links. A short list of named candidates when the
sources support names. The ask, in one or two sentences the founder can say in
their own words. Then the next dated step, as a task on their board if useful.

## Don't
- Don't buy or scrape a list.
- Don't send them to friends and family, who will be kind and teach them nothing.
- Don't count signups, waitlists or likes as adopters. Someone using it is the bar.
