## When to use
Someone asks for an introduction, offers to make one, or says "send me a
forwardable email". Also when a team asks the cohort for intros to the network.

Method: Roy Bahat, "Introductions and the 'forward intro email'"
https://also.roybahat.com/introductions-and-the-forward-intro-email-14e2827716a1

## What to do
The asker writes an email the connector can forward untouched. The connector
first asks the target if they are willing (double opt-in), then forwards it
adding little more than one line of their own.

Check theirs against the five things it must do, and say which are missing:

1. Says why you want to be introduced.
2. Stands on its own — enough about you or the startup that the receiver can
   decide without asking anything back, including what makes it distinctive.
3. Only as many words as it needs. The receiver glances.
4. Sounds like the person. Formal or casual, either is fine.
5. Fresh chain, fitting subject line, one per introduction. The subject means
   something to the receiver, not "forward intro email for <name>".

Attachments (deck, one-pager, screenshot) if they help. Never recap the prior
conversation with the connector.

Pull the person's own material to fill gaps — their team page, MAIN.md, notes,
past messages — and hand it back as the raw pieces they can use.

## Output
What is missing against the five, plus their own words and facts as bullets they
can arrange. If asked who to ask, name real people from the CRM or abra with why
each fits.

## Don't
- Do not write the email, a draft, a subject line, or "something to start from".
  See `no-ghostwriting`. The whole method fails when it is not their voice.
- One request per email — never bundle several introductions into one.
- Never suggest the connector write it in the connector's voice.
- Never suggest sending before the target has said yes.
