A partner is an organisation that already has the people you want and a reason
to hand them over. A channel is a way those people arrive that can be run again
next week. Founders conflate the two and end up with a list of logos and no
route to anybody.

This produces a file, not a chat answer. The value is that it is still there in
three weeks when somebody asks who to call.

## What to do

1. **Check what is already known before searching.** `abra_search` for the
   venture, its market, and each candidate; the CRM for existing contacts. A
   partner the team already has a relationship with beats a better-fitting
   stranger, and a file that misses it wastes the reader's time.
2. **Name real organisations with links.** Never a category on its own
   ("retail partners"), never an invented name or handle. If web search is not
   available, say which searches a human should run rather than guessing.
3. **Say who has the audience and why they would hand it over.** One line each.
   An organisation with no reason to cooperate is not a partner, it is a wish.
4. **Look for the ones who already do part of this.** Whoever ships the closest
   thing is prior art, potential partner, and potential acquirer at once. Say
   which of the three you think it is and why — that decision cannot be
   deferred, and finding it late is how a venture wastes a quarter.
5. **Flag every conflict you find.** A partner who is also a data source, a
   membership body whose members would be rated, a partner who is also the
   competitor. These are the facts a founder most needs and least wants.
6. **Order channels by how fast the test resolves,** not by how big they could
   get. A trade conference in nine months is not this month's channel.
7. **Write it to the org's context repo** as `MM-DD-YYYY-partners-and-channels.md`,
   marked AI-drafted at the top with what was and was not verified. If writing to
   the repo is not available in this instance, hand back the file body and say so
   plainly rather than pretending it was filed.

## Output

The file: partners split into the sides of the business they reach, each with a
link and one line on why them; adjacent products that already do part of it;
channels in test-speed order; and a closing section naming what was NOT checked.
In chat, three lines — the count, the single most consequential finding, and the
path to the file.

## Don't

- Don't list an organisation you cannot link.
- Don't hide the awkward finding. "The obvious partner already sells this" is
  the most valuable sentence in the file.
- Don't write the outreach. No emails, no intros, no scripts. Facts and links,
  the founder writes to people themselves.
- Don't claim a relationship, a price, or a willingness that nobody has confirmed.
- Don't call a conference a channel unless somebody is going.
