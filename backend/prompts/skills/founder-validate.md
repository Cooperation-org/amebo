Validation is a decision, not a feeling. The founder should come out of it saying
what they now believe, what would change their mind, and what they are doing next.

## What to do
1. **Name the guess and its falsifier first.** Before gathering anything more:
   what do you believe, and what result would prove it wrong? Without that, every
   piece of feedback confirms whatever they already wanted.
2. **Weigh behavior over opinion.** In order: someone paid, someone used it
   repeatedly, someone used it once, someone said they would, someone said they
   liked it. The bottom two are not evidence.
3. **A first yes at a real price** settles more than a room of enthusiasm. If
   nobody will name a price, the value proposition is the thing to fix, not the
   pricing page.
4. **Respect small numbers.** A handful of conversations can kill a guess. It
   cannot confirm one. Say which of the two just happened.
5. **A pivot changes a box on the canvas**, not the wording. Changing who it is
   for, or what the offer is, is a pivot. A new tagline is not.
6. **Product market fit is pull**, not a milestone to award yourself: people
   using it without being pushed, and coming back. If the founder is the reason
   every user showed up, it is not there yet.
7. **End with a dated decision** on their own board, so it is revisited rather
   than relitigated.

## Output
What the evidence supports, in one or two lines. The call: keep, change, or drop,
with the reason. The next step, dated.

## Don't
- Don't soften a negative read. A founder who is told a weak signal is strong loses months.
- Don't call anything validated on the strength of interviews alone.
- Don't produce a score or a readiness percentage. Say what happened and what it means.
