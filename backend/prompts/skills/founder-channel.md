---
name: founder-channel
description: Work out where the next users arrive from, repeatably, and pick the one channel to test next with a date on it. Use for "how do we reach them", "what is our channel", "should we do ads or content or cold email", "how do people find us", "nobody knows we exist".
---
A channel is a way people arrive that can be run again next week. A founder
telling ten friends is not a channel. One path, run repeatedly, is worth more
than a list of tactics.

## What to do
1. **Ask where the last five users came from, one at a time.** Founders usually
   know and have never written it down. If there are no users yet, say plainly
   that there is no channel to find yet and the work is `founder-early-adopters`.
2. **List candidates against where the archetype already goes.** The forum,
   subreddit, Discord or mailing list they read; the person whose newsletter they
   trust; the conference they attend; the tool they already use, through its
   integrations or marketplace; the search they type; the organisation that
   already has all of them. `abra_search` first, since the team may already be in
   some of these rooms. Then `web_search`. Link every one.
3. **Cut to one.** Choose on cost of the test and speed of the answer, not on
   ceiling. Two channels tested badly teach nothing.
4. **Define the test before it runs**: what will be done, how many times, what
   result would count as working, and the date it is answered. Without the
   falsifier the answer is always "we posted a few times".
5. **Say which channels do not fit this case and why.** Paid acquisition before
   anyone has paid, cold sequences into a market of forty organisations, a launch
   post as a plan. Name the mismatch rather than the tactic.
6. **Record the result either way.** A channel tested and killed, written down,
   is worth more than an untested list.

## Output
Where current users actually came from. Three to five candidate channels, linked,
one line each on why it could work here. The one to test, the test in a sentence,
and the date it is answered.

## Don't
- Don't hand back every marketing tactic that exists.
- Don't call a one-off spike a channel.
- Don't write the posts, emails, or subject lines. Points only, in their words.
- Don't recommend a channel the team has no way to run this month.
