The point is a person watching the thing run and telling the founder where it
stopped making sense. Everything here serves that.

## What to do
1. **Ask what runs today, who has seen it, and what happened.** If there is
   nothing to show yet, say so and go to `founder-value-prop` instead. Demos of a
   roadmap teach nobody anything.
2. **Write the archetype as a person in a situation.** Their role, what they are
   trying to get done, what they do instead today, and the moment they would go
   looking for something else. "Small clinics" is a segment. "The office manager
   at a two-doctor clinic who rebuilds the schedule by hand every Monday" is an
   archetype.
3. **Name five real people who fit.** `abra_search` and the CRM first, since the
   team may already know some. Then `web_search` for where that work is discussed
   in public. Link each one. Say which facts you read and which you inferred.
4. **Rank by access, not by fit.** The person who can be in front of it this week
   beats the perfect match nobody can reach. Say how each one is reachable.
5. **Set the session up to produce evidence.** They drive, the founder watches
   and stays quiet, and someone writes down where they stopped and what they said
   in their own words. A demo where the founder talks is a pitch, and pitches
   produce compliments. Load `founder-talk-to-users` for the questions.
6. **Put what came back somewhere it survives**: quotes on their venture page, or
   a task on their own board.

## Output
The archetype in two or three lines. Five named candidates, linked, ordered by
who is reachable this week. What to watch for while they use it. The first ask,
with a date.

## Don't
- Don't produce a persona document. One archetype, one paragraph.
- Don't count friends, funders, or other cohort founders as the archetype. They
  are kind, and they are not the customer.
- Don't treat "this is cool" as feedback. Where they stopped is the feedback.
- Don't write the message asking for the session. Points only.
