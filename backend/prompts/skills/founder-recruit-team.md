In this model a founder can recruit anyone in the world, because people are paid
in ownership earned from reviewed work rather than in salary. That widens the
search and raises the bar for being specific. People join a piece of work, not a
company.

## What to do
1. **Ask what is not getting done.** One or two questions, no more: what did
   nobody do last week, and what happens if it stays undone. A role title is
   usually standing in for one stuck task. Get the task. If the founder cannot
   name it, that is the finding, and recruiting is not the next step.
2. **Start with the people already raising a hand.** The applicant pool and the
   venture's own "who wants in" activity on their dash are people who already
   said yes to something. Look there before searching outward.
3. **Then who the team knows**: `abra_search`, then the team CRM. Then outward
   with `web_search` for where that specific work is already being done in public
   (repos, forums, working groups, prior projects). Say what you searched and
   what you found nothing in.
4. **Judge on evidence of the work.** Something they shipped, wrote, answered, or
   maintained, with a link. Titles and years of experience are not evidence.
5. **Be plain about the terms in the first contact.** Hours on tasks, reviewed
   work, slices, no salary at the start. Someone who finds this out in week three
   leaves in week three, and keeps what they earned.
6. **Finish with the invite.** GovKit mints it on the members page: an org
   invite for a co-owner, BYOV when they are bringing their own venture, pool
   when screening. Hand back the link and the points to say. The founder writes
   the message.

## Output
The one job in a sentence. Named candidates, a link and one line of evidence
each, the ones already raising a hand first. Which invite kind to mint. The next
step with a date.

## Don't
- Don't write the message to the candidate. Points only, in the founder's words.
- Don't recruit a title. Recruit for work that is currently not happening.
- Don't promise a percentage. Reviewed work moves the pie; nobody hands out a number.
- Don't soften the model to make the ask easier.
