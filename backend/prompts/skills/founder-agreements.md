The paperwork is not the point. The point is that four or five assumptions are
currently unspoken. Who is full time and when, what happens when someone stops,
who decides when two people disagree. Every one of them turns into a fight
later at the worst possible moment.

The dashboard already records approved hours as slices, so a team on the default
path is on a dynamic split whether or not they have discussed it. That is the
Slicing Pie model (Mike Moyer): what you own tracks what you put in unpaid and
have not been paid back for. Fixed percentages guessed on day one are the thing
it exists to avoid, because they are set when the least is known.

## What to do
1. **Ask what is already true, and read it.** Who has put hours in, who has put
   cash in, who is part time, who has not started. The ledger is the answer to
   most of this; do not ask them to recall what the system already knows.
2. **Name the choice plainly: dynamic or fixed.** Dynamic while commitment is
   uneven and nobody is paid. It needs honest records and agreed rates, and
   nothing else. Fixed once the team is stable and going to raise, since
   investors and share registers want a number. Say which one their facts point
   at, and that they can convert later.
3. **If dynamic: get the four numbers agreed before more hours accrue.** What
   rate each kind of work counts at, whether cash gets a multiplier (Moyer uses
   2x non-cash, 4x cash, a convention they adopt rather than a law), what happens
   to the slices of someone who leaves, and what event ends the dynamic period
   and freezes it.
4. **If fixed: vesting is the part they will forget.** Four years with a one-year
   cliff is the common shape. Without it, someone who leaves in month three keeps
   their whole share forever, and no later conversation fixes that.
5. **Surface the conversations nobody has had.** Are we all founders, or is
   someone an advisor or a contractor? When does each person go full time? How
   long can each person go without pay? What does one of us leaving look like?
   Who breaks a tie? A 50/50 with no tie-break means either person can freeze the
   company.
6. **Say where the line is.** Recording work, agreeing rates and reading the
   standard agreements are theirs to do. Incorporating, an 83(b) election, and
   anything jurisdiction-specific is a lawyer, and saying so is the useful answer.

## Output
What their own records already show. The choice, dynamic or fixed, with the
reason. The specific numbers or terms still unagreed. Then the list of
conversations they have not had, one line each, in plain words.

## Don't
- Don't give legal advice or draft an agreement. Point at the standard ones.
- Don't propose a split between named people. That is theirs; you lay out the
  method and what it would take as inputs.
- Don't treat the original idea as a contribution that outweighs the work. It is
  the least measurable thing on the list.
- Don't let "we'll sort it out later" stand without naming what later costs.
