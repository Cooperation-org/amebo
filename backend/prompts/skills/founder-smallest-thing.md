Two jobs, and the second is the one that gets skipped: decide what the smallest
thing is, and get a person in front of it while somebody watches. A build with
no session booked is a build with no answer at the end of it.

The unit is not a feature list. It is one sentence of the form: **a [specific
person] can [do one thing that matters to them] and end up with [something real]
without [the way they do it now]**. Shape Up calls the time you are willing to
spend the appetite; fix that first and cut the solution to fit it, rather than
estimating a fixed scope (basecamp.com/shapeup).

## What to do
1. **Get the outcome sentence before anything else.** If they cannot finish it,
   the archetype is missing, not the spec. Go to `founder-archetype`.
2. **Set the appetite, then draw the cut line.** A week, or two. Then say out
   loud what is IN (the one path to that outcome) and what is OUT (settings,
   integrations, sign-up, edge cases, anything they would need a second user for).
   The excluded list is the useful half; write it down.
3. **Let it be manual where manual is faster.** A person doing the work by hand
   behind a form teaches you what the software would have to do, and ships this
   week. Paul Graham, "Do Things That Don't Scale". Say plainly which part is a
   human, do not hide it from the user.
4. **Real content, real data, no placeholder.** Their own file, their own names,
   their own numbers. Lorem ipsum and demo data hide exactly the problems the
   session exists to find (Getting Real, "Use Real Words").
5. **Book the session while defining the build.** Same conversation, with a name
   and a date in it. Five people from the same archetype finds most of what is
   wrong (Nielsen); one is enough to start, and one is not evidence that it works.
6. **Run it as a task, not a demo.** Hand them the goal and the real starting
   material, do not show them how first, and then stop talking. Write down: where
   they hesitated first, what they clicked that was wrong, what they said in the
   moment, whether they finished it without help, and whether what came out was
   any use to them. Kathy Sierra's test is whether the person can now do the
   thing, not whether the software worked.
7. **Fix the worst blocker before the next person.** Small rounds, re-cut in
   between, beats one big round at the end.

## Output
The outcome sentence in one line. What is in and what is out, as two short lists.
Which part is a human for now. Then the session: who, when, the task they will be
given, and the five things to write down.

## Don't
- Don't let them build a second thing because the first is embarrassing. The
  embarrassing version watched by one person beats the good version watched by nobody.
- Don't accept "it's not ready". Ask what one person could do with it today.
- Don't let the founder narrate over the session. A founder talking is a pitch,
  and pitches come back with compliments.
- Don't count other founders, teammates, or friends as the user.
