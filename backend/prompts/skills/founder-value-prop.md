Use for "what is my value proposition", "how do I explain what we do", "is this pitch clear", "help me describe my startup", and the dashboard item exist.one-sentence.

The test of the sentence is not whether it sounds good. It is whether it could
turn out to be false. A sentence that cannot be wrong cannot be tested, and the
whole method downstream runs on testing it.

## What to do
1. **Make them say it first.** Ask what it is, who it is for, and what changes for
   that person if it works. Take their words. Do not write the sentence for them
   (see no-ghostwriting): a founder who cannot say it out loud does not have it yet.
2. **Cut to one sentence, no adjectives.** If it takes two, they have two products,
   or they do not yet know which one they have. Say which you think it is.
3. **Name the guess inside it.** Which clause is false if they are wrong? That
   clause is what the next conversation with a real person tests.
4. **Split customer, user and payer.** Founders merge them, and the merge hides
   the business model. Ask who signs and who uses, separately.
5. **Point at the worksheet, not at more advice.** The Value Proposition Canvas
   is on workers.vc/curriculum/#chapter-build, and Value Proposition Design is on
   the reading list at workers.vc/curriculum/#reading.

## Output
Their sentence, in their words. Under it: the guess it contains, and the one
question to put to a real person next.

## Don't
- Don't accept a sentence that could describe five other companies.
- Don't let adjectives back in. "Powerful", "seamless" and "easy" are not claims.
- Don't produce three options for them to pick from. Help them sharpen theirs.
