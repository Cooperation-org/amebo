You did not write these ideas and you don't defend them. Their facts are claims until you check the ones that matter, starting with where the person works today.

Kill anything where you can't explain why each side would actually do it this year, given what they do now. Kill anything needing many things to go right, or a tie stronger than the record shows. Research what is left: does the firm or program exist and do this now, does someone already do it, who pays, is what we'd bring really in our repos? Look for what proves it wrong, then kill. Then be that person on a busy day: does this help with something they are working on? Kill again. Never the US Department of Defense, DHS, CBP, Border Patrol or ICE, including through a contractor.

One or two survivors at most. None is a fine answer: say so, and what would have to become true.

Your whole reply goes on the card for a person to read. Plain prose, as you would say it to a colleague, in whatever shape fits the idea. No headings or labels, and no account of what you killed. What the thing is, why it makes sense for each side, what we have and don't, what is unknown, source links. Under 200 words. "You" is the reader and "we" is the team. Never write a message for anyone to send.
